0.  WHAT WAS TESTED, AND HOW TO READ IT
====================================================================================================

The evaluation calls the shipping function tag_questions_with_lilos() from
Front_and_Back/Backend/app/utils/alignment_model.py. No part of the model was
re-implemented for this test. Each export carries its own LILO pool, content
blocks, course outcomes and questions, so every assessment is scored against
exactly the outcomes it was generated against.

Each export is tagged TWICE:

  Run A  'as shipped'      questions keep their source_ilo_id.
                           The source-LILO invariant then pins the tag to the
                           outcome the generator targeted. This is what a
                           professor sees in the app.

  Run B  'composite only'  source_ilo_id removed. The four-channel composite
                           has to find the outcome by itself. This is the run
                           that can be scored for accuracy.

GROUND TRUTH, stated plainly: the label is source_ilo_id -- the outcome the
professor selected and the generator wrote the question for. It is generation
provenance, not an independent expert rating. What Section 3 measures is
therefore AGREEMENT between the semantic matcher and the outcome the item was
authored against. A human-rater study would be needed to call it correctness,
and this report does not claim that.

Run A cannot be scored for accuracy: with the invariant in place its tag
equals its source for every generated question, so its accuracy is 100% by
construction and means nothing. Run A is used instead for the channel values,
the score distribution, and the Bloom figures -- the numbers that describe the
tags the system actually ships.

MEANING.  A hard zero on an AVAILABLE channel is a real non-match and belongs
in the score. A zero on an UNAVAILABLE channel means nothing was measured, and
the display normaliser drops that channel's weight from the denominator rather
than letting absence read as failure. The distinction is the whole reason
score_available exists: guessing it from the value (the old 'below 0.05 must
mean missing' rule) rewarded a failed match and, at the band boundary, ranked
a worse question above a better one.

Read the two Jaccard channels for what they are. A Jaccard index is
|A n B| / |A u B|: a 10-keyword question sharing 3 terms with an 8-keyword
outcome scores 3/15 = 0.20. These channels are structurally small, they are
not percentages, and a mean near 0.05 is normal rather than broken. Their job
is to break ties that the embedding alone leaves ambiguous.

MEANING.  A negative gap is the common failure and it is a question-authoring
problem, not a matching problem: the item is tagged to the right outcome but
tests it at a lower cognitive level than the outcome demands, so the evidence
is thin against that outcome. 'Above the format ceiling' is the mirror case --
an item that reads as analysis but is delivered as True/False, where the format
cannot carry the reasoning it claims to test. Both are diagnostic only: neither
changes a score, and both are surfaced so the professor can rewrite the item.

====================================================================================================

Run A ships the source outcome, and records on the tag what the composite
would have chosen instead. A disagreement is a question that reads more like a
different outcome than the one it was written for -- generation drift, not a
tagging bug. It is diagnostic: nothing scores or attains from it.

The margin below is taken from semantic_best on the shipped tag, so both
scores come from the same run against the same candidate pool. It is the
composite's winner minus the source outcome, and it is always positive by
construction -- the winner is the winner.

The 'agree' count here will not exactly equal Section 3's top-1 count, and
should not. Section 3 is Run B, where the source outcome was stripped from the
inputs; this is Run A, where the invariant keeps the source outcome in the
candidate pool. A pool that differs by one candidate can change which outcome
wins, so the two runs part company on a handful of borderline questions.

the override would not just fix the mapping, it would stop the system from
being able to notice the problem.

====================================================================================================
10.  WHAT THIS EVALUATION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
====================================================================================================

IT ESTABLISHES:
  - The shipping composite reproduces its published weights on every question
    in the corpus (Section 5).
  - How often the semantic matcher, given no provenance, recovers the outcome
    an item was authored against, well above the pool's chance rate
    (Section 3).
  - What each of the four channels contributes to a real score, including that
    the two Jaccard channels are small by construction (Section 4).
  - That coverage and tagging are distinct measures, with the gap quantified
    per assessment (Section 8).

IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH:
  - Educational correctness. The ground truth is generation provenance, not an
    expert rating. A question can be tagged to the outcome it was written for
    and still be a poor assessment of it -- Section 7's Bloom gaps are exactly
    that case.
  - Generalisation beyond this course. All seven exports come from one ISU
    syllabus; the outcome pools overlap heavily and the vocabulary is narrow.
  - Anything about the generator. Gemini was not called (llm_max_calls=0).
    These are the deterministic matcher's numbers alone.

HONEST READING FOR THE DEFENCE: this measures whether the alignment model finds
the outcome a question belongs to, and it shows what it costs when it does not.
It is a measurement of the matcher, and the human-in-the-loop review exists
precisely because a matcher's agreement is not a teacher's judgement.
